Renude · 2026

An AI beauty concierge that lives inside the shop, not beside it.

The brief was a skin-analysis revamp. Research turned it into something larger. In ten weeks I researched, designed, engineered, and user-tested a concierge plugin for beauty retailers: 22 ways in from the storefront, a browser-side agent with 27 tools, and answers that show their work. I handed it to the team as a live prototype.

How the brief moved

Renude makes skin consultation tools that beauty retailers install on their sites: a quiz with photo analysis, and a chat that recommends routines. Week one's brief was to revamp the analysis.

Auditing the live assistant on a retailer's site, screen by screen, showed the biggest losses weren't inside the analysis but around it. The tool was hard to find, mistaken for customer support, forgot the shopper between pages, and couldn't talk about the product on screen. By week four the question had become: where does AI enter the shopping journey, and how does it earn trust once it's there?

Research

Market

Seven products on one grid

Most beauty AI was hard to discover, quiz-heavy, and over-promised on intelligence. Few turned answers into anything a shopper could keep.

Journey audit

Every friction point, pinned

I used the live assistant end to end, marked where it broke or stalled, then watched testers think aloud through the same flow.

Interviews

Engineering, skin practice, science

They set the rules: reassure without overclaiming, explain the why in a warm sentence, and never infer a shade from a photo.

A store to test it in

Three weeks in Figma couldn't answer the real questions, which were about behavior over time: whether a self-typing ask bar reads as an invitation, or whether a panel that follows you across pages feels like a companion. So I built Solar, a fictional retailer with product pages, a bag, checkout, search, filters, and 72 products across skin, makeup, and hair, then installed the concierge on top of it. Testers used ideas instead of hearing them described.

Architecture

The concierge is a plugin layered on the host store. Only two adapter files touch the retailer, and retailer pages only know a named slot, the same contract as a Shopify theme app extension. Turned off, it leaves zero Renude strings in the page.

The agent runs in the browser: the model decides what the shopper meant and how to phrase it, while tools run against live state, so the profile and bag never leave the device. A stateless proxy holds the key, builds the system prompt server-side, validates input, rate-limits per IP, and streams the response back. Tool schemas are generated from the browser's own definitions at build time, so they can't drift.

Answers that show their work

Testers distrusted a tool that answered the same question two ways, and a retailer's own assistant starts out suspected of selling. So the model never states a fact it wasn't handed. Every price, ingredient, and verdict comes from a tool, and cards are drawn by tool runs. The profile reaches tools through a proxy that records which answers each run actually read, so "read from your profile" is a measurement, not the model's account of itself. With no profile saved, it says nothing about "your" skin.

What I built

Entry points

22 doors, each measured

An ask bar, product-card signals, a product-page question block, a "Best for my skin" sort option, a bag review. Each fires seen and clicked separately, because "nobody noticed it" and "nobody cared" need opposite fixes.

Skills

Capabilities with names

Nine skills on a rail that follows the aisle and the conversation. A skill fills the composer instead of sending, so the shopper stays the one who decides.

Consultation depth

Quick, Balanced, Full

One control changes the register of the answer and re-tiers every card already in the thread, without asking again.

Memory

Products, stamps, notes

Saved items, "not for me" stamps, and notes in the shopper's own words that the concierge reads back, synced to the retailer's wishlist through the adapter.

Review summaries

Two phases that can't disagree

Stats are computed first and prose is written over them; the merge step rejects any prose containing a digit. Generated once, stored, and read, never inferred at page view.

Voice

Dictation that works everywhere

Browser speech worked for one tester and hung for the next, so audio is transcribed on the server and lands in the box for review. It never sends on its own.

What testing changed

  • Structured answers read as honest. The same recommendation as a card felt less like a sales pitch than as a paragraph, so cards replaced prose everywhere.
  • Corner bubbles got ignored as promo-code widgets and pop-ups got closed on instinct, so the launcher was quieted and the ask bar moved above the hero.
  • Features went unused until they had names. A skills menu behind a plus button was invisible; a rail of named skills became something testers came back for.
  • People wanted the panel bigger, which produced a resizable drawer, a movable window, and a draggable phone sheet.
  • Saving was the surprise. Nobody asked for it in the brief and every tester reached for it, which became the Products tab.

What I learned

Show a conclusion, not an invitation.

Entry points that offered to help were walked past. The ones that had already worked something out, like a chip saying "worth a closer look," got used.

Trust comes from provenance and consistency.

Testers believed an answer when they could see which of their own answers it used. Being traceable and predictable mattered more than being clever.

Depth is a register, not a word count.

A quick answer and a full one differ in which words are used and what gets named. Once that was clear, one control replaced a set of persona ideas.

Motion should explain a change.

A grid that visibly reorders reads as the tool having done something. A fake "analyzing" pause reads as theater.
